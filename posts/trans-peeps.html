Gender Anthropics: An Omissive Lie

August 17, 2026 x min read

Recently, a conversation with my father, an anatomical pathologist, centered around morality of trans people. Many tense dialogues arise from my status as a trans women, however, I find insight about the traditional conservative—the archetypal Mitt Romney supporter—via these talks: be forewarned if you are uncomfortable with the topic. Particularly, he commented about the social perception of others, which provoked some thoughts.

For context, I inquired about why he views hormone replacement therapy as immoral: his response is not the notable part, and I tend to conclude that the moralization often arises from disgust; more salient was an addendum comment after he became frustrated. In essence, he stated that my appearance, as a passing trans woman, serves as a lie: the imitation of form deceives others omissively. Now, I agree with a generic feminist critique here: his view overlaps with the "panic defense," rooted within objectification of women and misogynistic corollaries. However, abstraction of these definitions lead to worthwhile implications about transphobia.

You do not have ovaries. Your outer self embodies a lie [from exogenous hormones].

Fix a population which believes traits a, b, c implies facts x, y, z. Consider a person with traits b, c, d who both can and wishes to imitate traits a, b, c. Upon learning of this person, thier imitation becomes viewed as deception to the population: it provides a counterfactual to the implication.
